Post op, considering findings...With stealthy exploitation no longer necessary as part of any active measurement cycles, prioritizing ticketing of outstanding vulnerabilities can start the technical remediation process while other findings are analyzed and reports are prepared. But simply remediating isn't enough &mdash; consideration should be given to patterns or abnormalities stemming from lapses in technical preventative controls.

When I finish an operation and transition to reporting, I begin by gathering my findings and preparing recommendations. My first pass typically involves noting the technical gaps that allowed me to progress in my attack chains. Then, in my second pass, I step back and ask "why?" several times. This helps me identify root causes that allowed for a technical flaw to occur in the first place. This is the most common way I identify process failures. Though I can't always be certain at the root cause, more often than not, my suspicions are confirmed in discussions with relevant security teams.

## Scaling Outcomes

A common mentoring lesson that helps juniors and seniors increase their impact is that as you progress in seniority, your sphere of influence grows. So while junior engineers would develop tools that aid their immediate team, senior engineers would build tools that benefit with other teams, and Staffs/Principals would be delivering impact and value across the company. The same goes towards people managers, but being a technical lead myself, I'll refrain from commenting there.

One easy way to scale the impact of your work is to share useful information with other teams who may be able to utilize it to improve their own business deliverables. As I showed in my business impact-focused bullets above, you can often continue to squeeze out value from a project by collaborating with other teams to continue producing results and improving security. This should be done carefully though, as you don't want to expand too far into other teams' responsibilities.

At the conclusion of a project, it's common to walk away with greater domain and tribal knowledge on that topic, and that can be very useful to other teams who may not be aware of findings or lessons learned that are relevant to them. This is especially true for red teaming, as there are often many more stakeholders than just the owners of the target systems from the operation. Sharing your understanding and thoughts with teams that may find value in your information could inspire other teams to take on or prioritize new projects.
